# Trading caveats

A regime model becomes a strategy only after a fully specified policy maps an available probability
to an order. Backtest that policy with the same information and operational constraints it would
have had live.

## Minimum specification

- Decision timestamp, data cutoff, signal lag, order timestamp, execution price, and refit cadence.
- Probability-to-position rule, abstention/hysteresis, leverage, sizing, and risk constraints.
- Commissions, bid–ask spread, slippage, borrow, funding, market impact, and turnover.
- Tradable universe as known then, liquidity/capacity limits, corporate actions, and delistings.
- Benchmark, cash return, rebalancing calendar, and treatment of missing/stale signals.

Use filtered probabilities generated by fold-specific parameters for live claims. Smoothed states can
stratify a diagnostic chart but cannot drive simulated trades. Lag all decisions, fit preprocessing
and model parameters within each walk-forward fold, tune only in inner validation, and keep a final
test locked. Ensure label horizons, lookbacks, and positions do not overlap folds without purging.

## Reading results

Report gross and net returns, turnover, exposure, volatility, drawdown, tail loss, capacity, and
sensitivity to costs and delays. Inspect performance by fold, asset, calendar period, and state.
Annualized Sharpe is not IID evidence, maximum drawdown is one realized path, and a small number of
state transitions gives little effective sample size. Attractive results can arise from data mining,
implicit volatility timing, beta, leverage, or one crisis.

Stress higher costs, delayed execution, missed fills, stale data, parameter drift, and a never-seen
state. Paper trading and monitored shadow deployment should precede capital. Regime identification
does not guarantee forecastability, and forecastability does not guarantee net profitability.
